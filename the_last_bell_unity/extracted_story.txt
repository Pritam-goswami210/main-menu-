The School That Should Have Been Empty

The school had been closed for years.
Everyone in the area knew that.
The windows were broken. The paint had peeled away from the walls. Grass had swallowed the
path leading to the entrance.
Nobody attended classes there anymore.
But there was one thing people still talked about.
The bell.
Some said that on certain nights, exactly at midnight, the old school bell could still be heard.
Most people laughed when they heard the story.
Pritam did not.
Neither did Ahil.
Nor Arghadeep, Shubhodeep, or Ahaan.
They had heard the story too many times.
And one evening, they decided to find out whether it was true.

                                                                   ·

They reached the school shortly before midnight.
The building stood at the end of a narrow road, surrounded by trees.
There was no electricity.
No security guard.
No students.
No teachers.
Nothing.
At least, that was what they thought.
Pritam pushed the old gate.
It opened with a long metallic groan.

The five friends looked at each other.
Then they walked inside.
The Corridor

The main corridor was darker than they expected.
Their phone flashlights illuminated only small pieces of the building.
A classroom door.
A broken window.
An old notice board.
A clock.
Everything looked frozen in time.
Ahil pointed toward the wall.
There were old photographs hanging there.
Most were covered in dust.
Some had fallen from their frames.
One photograph was different.
It showed a group of students standing in front of the school.
Five students.
Pritam stared at it.
There was something strange about the photograph.
There were actually six shadows.

                                                                    ·

"Probably just the lighting," Arghadeep said.
Nobody answered.
At the end of the corridor stood an old classroom.
The door was slightly open.
Pritam pushed it.
The hinges screamed.

THE LAST BELL                                   7

Inside were rows of wooden desks.
A blackboard covered most of the front wall.
And above the teacher's desk was an old clock.
The clock had stopped.
11:59.
The Classroom

The classroom smelled of dust and old wood.
A few desks were still arranged in rows.
One chair stood beside the teacher's desk.
A school bell rested on the desk.
Nobody had touched it.
Yet Pritam could hear something.
A faint ticking.
He looked at the clock.
11:59.
Tick.
Tick.
Tick.
The second hand moved.
The clock had started again.

                                                                    ·

Ahaan stepped backward.
"Did anyone else see that?"
The clock reached twelve.
The school bell rang.
Once.
The sound travelled through the entire building.
Then silence.
Pritam's phone went black.
Every phone went black.

                                                              8
THE LAST BELL                                        9

And somewhere in the corridor, a door slammed shut.
Attendance

A voice came from the corridor.
Quiet.
Almost like a teacher speaking from another classroom.
"Attendance."
Nobody moved.
The voice continued.
"Arghadeep."
Arghadeep's face went pale.
"Present."
He immediately covered his mouth.
"I didn't say that."
The voice continued.
"Shubhodeep."
Silence.
Then a whisper came from somewhere behind them.
"Present."
Shubhodeep turned around.
Nobody was there.
"Ahaan."
"Present."
Ahaan had not spoken.
Ahil grabbed Pritam's arm.
"Pritam."
The voice stopped.
The classroom became completely silent.
Then the teacher's chair moved.

By itself.

               ·

A piece of paper fell from the desk.
It was an old attendance sheet.
Five names were written on it.
Underneath them was a sixth line.
The name had been scratched away.
Pritam picked up the paper.
Under the scratches, he could barely make out a few letters.
They looked like a name.
But nobody knew whose name it was.

The ID Cards

They searched the classroom.
Inside an old wooden cabinet they found several school ID cards.
Most were damaged.
Some had photographs.
Some had names.
One card had no photograph.
Another had no name.
Pritam picked it up.
The moment his fingers touched it, the classroom lights flickered.
For a fraction of a second, someone appeared in the reflection of the window.
A student.
Standing behind them.

                                                                   ·

They turned.
Nothing.
The reflection was gone.
Pritam looked at the ID card again.
A name had appeared.
He dropped it.
The card hit the floor.
The name was the same name that had been scratched from the attendance sheet.

The Recording

Behind the teacher's desk they discovered an old cassette recorder.
It was covered in dust.
There was a cassette inside.
Ahaan pressed play.
Static filled the classroom.
Then a teacher's voice.
"Class, remain seated."
The recording continued.
"We have one student missing."
Static.
Then screaming.
The recording stopped.
Nobody spoke.
A few seconds later, the recorder started again.
This time there was no teacher.
There was a child's voice.
"Why did they forget me?"

                                                                    ·

The five friends heard footsteps outside.
Slow footsteps.
One.
Two.
Three.
They stopped directly outside the classroom.

THE LAST BELL                                    14

The door handle moved.
But nobody opened the door.
Then the footsteps continued down the corridor.
The Hidden Passage

The five friends decided to leave.
But the corridor was no longer the same.
The door they had entered through was gone.
The walls seemed longer.
The classrooms were in different places.
Pritam noticed something behind the blackboard.
A small gap.
They moved the board.
Behind it was a narrow doorway.
A staircase descended into darkness.

                                                                   ·

They followed it.
The walls were covered with old writing.
Names.
Hundreds of them.
Some had been crossed out.
Some were almost impossible to read.
At the bottom was a small room.
Inside was another attendance register.
It was newer than everything else.
The first page contained the names of the five friends.
Pritam.
Ahil.
Arghadeep.

The Last Bell

The bell rang again.
This time it was much louder.
The entire school seemed to shake.
The register turned its own pages.
A blank page appeared.
A sixth name slowly appeared on it.
The same forgotten name.
Pritam looked toward the doorway.
A figure stood there.
A student.
Head lowered.
Uniform covered in dust.
The figure raised its face.
There was no expression.
Only empty darkness where the eyes should have been.
The voice whispered:
"You remembered me."
The lights went out.

                                                                    ·

When they came back on, the figure was gone.
The hidden room was empty.
The staircase was gone.
And the five friends were standing outside the school gate.
Morning had arrived.

Nobody remembered leaving.
Pritam looked back at the building.
The old clock in the classroom window showed exactly 12:00.
The bell rang one final time.
Then silence.

The Return

For several days, none of them talked about the school.
They tried to convince themselves it had been a dream.
Then Pritam received a photograph.
It was a picture of the five friends standing outside the school.
The photograph had been taken the night they entered.
Pritam stared at it.
There were six people in the photograph.

                                                                   ·

The sixth person stood behind them.
Closer than before.
Pritam showed the others.
Nobody knew what to say.
They returned to the school the following night.
This time, they went prepared.
Flashlights.
Batteries.
A camera.
The old ID card.
And the attendance sheet.
They wanted answers.

The Names Beneath

The school was exactly as they remembered it.
Except for one thing.
The photographs on the corridor wall were different.
There were more students.
More faces.
More shadows.
Pritam looked closer.
The faces had been scratched out.
One by one.
They followed the corridor until they reached the classroom.
The clock was still running.
11:59.

                                                                    ·

Ahil found writing underneath one of the desks.
A sentence had been carved into the wood.

                                        THEY DID NOT FORGET ME.

                                                THEY ERASED ME.

Pritam understood.
The student had not simply disappeared.
Someone had deliberately removed every trace of them.
Their photograph.
Their name.
Their records.

THE LAST BELL  22

Their memory.
Everything.
The Attendance Room

The hidden doorway appeared again.
This time the staircase was longer.
Much longer.
They descended.
At the bottom they discovered a room filled with old registers.
Years of attendance records.
Hundreds of pages.
Hundreds of names.
Some students had disappeared from the records.
Others had been replaced.
But every register contained one strange pattern.
The same sixth line.
Always scratched away.

                                                                    ·

Arghadeep opened the oldest register.
The paper was almost black with age.
The first page contained six names.
Five were crossed out.
The sixth was still there.
Ahaan whispered:
"That's impossible."
A bell rang above them.
The register closed by itself.
Then every light in the room turned on.

                                                             23
THE LAST BELL                           24

The walls were covered in photographs.
All of them showed the same student.
The forgotten student.
Watching.
Waiting.
The Sixth Figure

The figure appeared at the end of the room.
This time, it spoke clearly.
"I waited."
Nobody moved.
"I waited for someone to remember."
Pritam stepped forward.
"Who are you?"
The figure pointed toward the oldest register.
"My name."
Pritam looked down.
The name was visible now.
It had never been destroyed.
It had only been hidden beneath layers of ink.

                                                                    ·

The figure explained that many years earlier, a student had disappeared from the school.
The adults had decided to erase the incident.
The attendance records were changed.
Photographs were removed.
The student's belongings were destroyed.
Eventually, everyone stopped talking about them.
But the school remembered.
Every night, the bell called the forgotten student back.
Every night, attendance was taken.
And every night, there was one empty place.

THE LAST BELL                               26

Until five new names entered the register.
The Final Attendance

The clock reached midnight.
The bell rang.
Once.
The figure turned toward the register.
"Attendance."
Pritam heard his name.
"Present."
Ahil answered.
"Present."
Arghadeep answered.
"Present."
Shubhodeep answered.
"Present."
Ahaan answered.
"Present."
Silence.
Then the teacher's voice spoke.
"Sixth student."
Nobody answered.
The figure looked at them.
Pritam finally understood.
The school did not want another victim.
It wanted a witness.
Someone to remember.

                                                                    ·

THE LAST BELL                                     28

Pritam spoke the forgotten name aloud.
The bell stopped.
The lights went out.
For several seconds there was complete darkness.
Then the clock began moving again.
12:01.
The school had finally moved past midnight.
Thank You for Remembering Me

The five friends walked out of the school.
Behind them, every classroom door slowly opened.
One after another.
The corridor filled with the sound of chairs moving.
Then came footsteps.
Hundreds of them.
But nobody followed.
The school bell rang once more.
Only once.
Pritam looked back.
The sixth figure stood in the classroom window.
For the first time, the figure smiled.
Then it disappeared.

                                                                   ·

The next morning, the school looked different.
The old photographs were gone.
The attendance registers were blank.
The hidden staircase no longer existed.
And the old ID card had changed.
There was now a photograph.
A name.
And beneath it, one sentence.

                "THANK YOU FOR REMEMBERING ME."
